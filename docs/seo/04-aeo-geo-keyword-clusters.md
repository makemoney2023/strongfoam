## Cluster A — Commercial spray foam (pillar)

**Primary:** commercial spray foam insulation Ontario  
**Secondaries / H2s:** ICI spray foam contractor; multi-unit residential spray foam; closed cell spray foam commercial; open cell spray foam commercial; spray foam wall assembly; thermal barrier over spray foam Ontario  

### AEO Q&As (draft — verify facts before publish)

**Q: What is commercial spray foam insulation used for?**  
**A:** On ICI and multi-unit projects it is used to air-seal and insulate wall, roof, and cavity assemblies as part of the building envelope. Strong Foam Insulation Inc. applies SPF as a specialty trade alongside related fire-protection and AVB scopes where specified.

**Q: Do you only do residential attic foam?**  
**A:** Strong Foam’s public positioning is ICI and multi-unit residential. Some custom residential work exists, but commercial and mid/high-rise packages are the core focus. Request an estimate with your drawings for fit.

**Q: Open-cell or closed-cell for a steel-stud wall?**  
**A:** It depends on the assembly, vapour control strategy, and the project specification. Closed-cell is often chosen where rigidity and lower vapour permeance matter; open-cell where fill and acoustics matter. Final choice follows the design professional’s spec and manufacturer TDS — not a website guess.

## Cluster B — Fireproofing / PFP

**Primary:** cementitious fireproofing Ontario  
**Secondaries:** spray applied fireproofing contractor; structural steel fireproofing; Monokote applicator Ontario; passive fire protection high-rise; fireproofing inspection  

### AEO Q&As

**Q: What is cementitious fireproofing?**  
**A:** It is a spray-applied cementitious coating used to protect structural steel for a specified fire-resistance rating, installed to the project’s listed design and manufacturer instructions (for example GCP Monokote-class products when specified).

**Q: Cementitious or intumescent?**  
**A:** Cementitious PFP and intumescent coatings solve related but different finish, thickness, and exposure problems. The specification, member type, and architectural finish usually decide. Strong Foam works with both cementitious and intumescent product families commonly specified in Ontario commercial work.
